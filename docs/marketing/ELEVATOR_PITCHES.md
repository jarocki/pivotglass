# Elevator pitches

Draft spoken introductions. Adapt the final invitation to the listener's task.

## For an analyst

Pivotglass helps you turn a clue into a judgment you can explain. Keep your
question, collection work, source provenance, relationships, and competing
hypotheses in one local case workspace. Use the graphs to inspect connections
and the notebook to test what those connections mean. You choose what to
collect and what to conclude. Start with the offline case to see the whole
workflow before connecting a provider.

## For a team lead

Pivotglass makes investigative reasoning easier to inspect and discuss. It
connects a question to the sources, hypotheses, contradictions, gaps, and report
that support an analyst's judgment. New analysts get guided question framing
and reflection during work; experienced analysts can use lighter guidance.
The aim is better handoffs and more deliberate practice. A pilot should measure
whether it improves your team's outcomes; the software does not certify
expertise or promise a productivity percentage.

## For an evaluator or implementer

Pivotglass is a local investigation workspace with a browser cockpit and shared
terminal interfaces. Its core uses deterministic collection and evidence
records; optional models provide synthesis under analyst control. Evaluate the
synthetic offline case, review data handling and capacity, then qualify the
providers and workflows you need. External integrations have explicit preview
boundaries. The implementation and operations guides explain setup, recovery,
and a bounded pilot before wider use.

## One sentence

Pivotglass connects the investigative question, evidence, competing explanations,
and report in a local workspace so an analyst's reasoning can be inspected.
